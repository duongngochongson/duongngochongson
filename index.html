Hear Them Out: the complaint call

A short course for community managers at the front desk of Cozy, a fictional coworking space. The learner takes a complaint call from Sofia, a member, using HEARD — Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve, Diagnose — then logs the case so the next shift can act on it and Sofia hears back on the day she was promised.

Take the course ↗
ROLE
Design + build
FORMAT
Branching call + simulated tool
LEARNERS
Front-desk community managers
TOOLS
React · XState · Motion · Gemini

THE PROBLEM

Members give Cozy 2.7 out of 5 for how it handles complaints — the lowest score in the member survey. On the phone, managers explain the rules before asking what happened, apologise in general terms and end with “we'll look into it”: no options, no date. About half of the logged complaints have a next step, and some members wait weeks.

WHAT I DID

Built an action map and sorted the barriers by type; process gaps — no owner, no follow-up date — went to managers instead of into the course.
Turned HEARD into five decisions inside one real-time call. Every weaker option is a near miss: something right next to one familiar habit.
Made consequences visible: Sofia's mood shifts line by line, and a week later her survey says 1, 2, 3 or 5 out of 5 — with a debrief and a rewind to where it turned.
Part 2 moves to Cozy Desk: log the case from your own call note, hand it over at shift change, call back on the day you promised.
Three practices afterwards: a new call with an AI-played member, the private talk, and a write-up checked by an AI grader.

THE GOAL & THE BUILD

2.7 → 3.8
target complaint score, over two quarters
5 + 9
decisions and log fields scored on the first try
320px
works on a phone: single taps, captions for every sound

Cozy is a fictional client: the numbers are design goals from the needs analysis, not measured results.

Not-Home Delivery Stop

A 7-slide eLearning module for city-route drivers: what to do when nobody answers the door. It runs on the web and ships as a SCORM 1.2 package for an LMS.

Open the module ↗
ROLE
Design + build
FORMAT
7-slide scenario module
LEARNERS
City-route delivery drivers
TOOLS
React · XState · Zod · Piper TTS

THE PROBLEM

On not-home stops, fewer than half of parcels get delivered on the first attempt. Drivers skip the call to protect their stop rate, aren't sure what SIG and AGE parcels allow, and give in to requests outside the rules — like leaving a parcel that needs a signature at the door.

WHAT I DID

Action-mapped four on-the-job actions; two barriers were fixed outside training — the handling code on the arrival screen, and neighbour handovers counted as success on the driver scorecard.
Gave each action its own practice: drag to order, sort the parcels, a voiced phone call, a hotspot to find the safe place, and a branching run of two stops.
Added a free-text call with an AI customer, and a separate AI grader that quotes the learner's own words against the three call moves.
Kept the four job aids as the single source, so the cards, the scoring, the AI prompts and the diagrams can't drift apart.
Accessible from the start: keyboard and tap alternatives to every drag, captions on every line, axe checks on every slide at four screen widths.

THE GOAL & THE BUILD

45% → 65%
target first-attempt success, within three months
7
interaction types, one per slide
SCORM 1.2
LMS package with resume and pass/fail, plus the web version

Fictional client: the numbers are design goals, not measured results.
