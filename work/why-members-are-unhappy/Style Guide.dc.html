This guide sets one visual and interaction system for Hear Them Out, a course for the front-desk staff of Cozy, whom Cozy calls community managers. Cozy is a business with three coworking spaces, which are shared workplaces where members rent desks and rooms on monthly plans. Cozy, its members and its staff are fictional and were created for this portfolio.

The course is a branching scenario, in which each choice the learner makes changes what happens next. In Part 1, the learner takes a complaint call from Sofia, a member who calls because another member made a long phone call next to her in Focus, the silent zone. During the call, the learner keeps their own note of it. In Part 2, the learner logs the case in Cozy Desk, a practice copy of the software Cozy's front desk uses, and follows it up. Every path ends with Sofia's answer in Cozy's member survey, a score from 1/5 to 5/5. Son, a guide character, stays with the learner through the course. He talks in chat messages until the phone turns over, and after that from a bar at the top of the screen, called Son's bar.

After the course, the learner can try three practices, which are not scored. In the practice call, an AI plays Omar, another member, and a second AI checks the call against the criteria. In the private talk, the learner talks with Dan, the member who made the call next to Sofia. In the written practice, the learner writes a log entry, which an AI checks. The practices do not count toward passing, partly because AI scoring can vary.

The same system covers the pieces that belong to Cozy itself, which this guide calls Cozy's world: Cozy Desk, the job aid cards (HEARD, Options, the log card and the Wi-Fi Down card) and Cozy in 60 seconds, a short guide for new members, made for phones. Every value comes from the built course: the tokens and base styles in src/design, the components in src/components and the screens' stylesheets. The Wi-Fi Down card and Cozy in 60 seconds were made outside the course, with the same values.

Terms

The course's screens have two layers. The course layer is the dark desk that fills every screen, and everything the course puts on it, such as the phone, the conversation and Son's messages.

  • Every screen, the top bar included, has the same dark ground, like a desk at night: night #1F1A12.
  • Text sits on light boxes, like paper under a lamp: paper #FBF7EE, ink text, 16px corners and the raise shadow. Speech bubbles, decision cards, the note, the intro cards and the results all sit on paper.
  • Large headings sit on the dark ground in the paper color. Long text on the dark ground is night thought #E8DFD0, and secondary text is night soft #CFC4B1.
  • The phone is dark too: a night deep screen #120F0A in a night surface frame #2A2318.
  • Son's messages do not sit on paper, so that his words look different from a member's. Each is a dark box with a 1.5px dashed outline in line strong #8C8170, and his line is set in the paper color, in upright type.
  • Events, such as "Call ended", show as pill-shaped chips on the dark ground, in mono.
  • The lamp #F2A93B is the light on the desk. Its amber marks the disc at the start, the focus ring, progress, Son's button, the rings around a ringing phone, and a 4px lamp edge on a note on paper.

Cozy's world is everything that carries the Cozy logo or comes from Cozy's own software and printed material: Cozy Desk, the member card with Sofia's details, the case status box (where the learner sets the status of the case after the call), the open cases list, the reminder, the survey card with Sofia's answer, the desk computer's sign-in screen and the job aid cards.

  • Cozy's world uses the light theme (data-cz-theme="light"), also where it sits on the dark desk. It has paper surfaces, ink text, desk #F4EFE4 for quiet areas, line #E3DACB for dividers, mono for the system's voice, and zone colors for wayfinding.
  • Cozy Desk is a light software window on the desk: paper, 16px corners, the pop shadow, and a header with the symbol, "Cozy Desk" and "On shift: [name]" with the lamp dot.
  • Cozy's boxes in the course carry the logo at their top: the symbol on the member card and the case status box, the lockup on the survey card.
  • The Wi-Fi Down card and Cozy in 60 seconds belong to Cozy's world. Each is set on paper, with ink text and mono labels, and wherever a zone color appears, the zone's icon and name come with it. Son, his dashed box and the dark ground belong to the course layer, so they do not appear in either piece.
  • Cozy in 60 seconds is built from the course's wayfinding parts. Its zone map is the FloorPlan, with each room in its tint and border, its icon and its name, and an amber disc for where the reader is. Its signs are drawn with CozySign: the Focus sign with its alternative ("Silent zone. Calls and meetings in Flex →"), the floor tape, the desk card, and the fridge and shelf labels for the kitchen. The guide shows one screen at a time in a single column, inside a phone frame up to 392px wide.

Contrast is the WCAG 2 contrast ratio (see Terms), computed by src/design/contrast.js against the course's paper #FBF7EE and ground #1F1A12. A test, tokens.test.js, checks that text reaches 4.5:1 and that borders and icons reach 3:1. The course's paper is #FBF7EE, set in base.css. It is a little warmer than #FFFCF5, the paper color that the design system's files (tokens.json and tokens.css) keep. New pieces use #FBF7EE. The logo drawings in this guide keep #FFFCF5 for their paper letters, as the design system's files draw them. Fills are flat colors: a test rejects gradients, and it rejects colors typed into a stylesheet in place of a token.

Ink and text

The lamp

The guide (Son). Son is drawn in the dark ground's own colors: his box is --cz-night with a 1.5px dashed --cz-line-strong outline, his name tag --cz-night-soft, his line --cz-paper. His button is --cz-night-raised with a 1.5px --cz-lamp border, a paper label and a lamp icon. His face sits on a paper disc.

Feedback. Each result shows an icon and a word together: the right icon (a circled tick) with "Right" or "Met", the notyet icon (a turning arrow) with "Not yet", and the alert icon with an error message.

Call states

Zones. Each Cozy space has three work zones: Focus for silent work, Flex for desk work, calls and meetings, and Private for closed rooms. There is also a shared kitchen. Zone colors are for wayfinding: they show which zone a place or a case is in. A zone color comes with the zone's icon and name, and it is not used for decoration or in the logo. Zone bands are stacked together in one case, as a key (a legend) to every zone, with each band labeled. Each zone color reaches 4.8:1 or more as text on its own tint, the light version of the color.

The course also uses these styles, which are not steps of the scale:

  • Son's line is IBM Plex Sans 500, 16 / 24, in the paper color, upright.
  • Names are Archivo 600: 28px on the phone (32px while it rings), 22px on the member card and 20px on the call bar. The course title in the top bar is Archivo 600 at 18px.
  • HEARD letters are Archivo 700 at 52px. Job aid titles are Archivo 700 at 28px, with a line height of 1.05 and -0.02em tracking (letter spacing), and the keys on a job aid are Archivo 700 at 17px, in amber ink.
  • Captions are IBM Plex Mono 500 at 17px, with a line height of 1.3.

Rules for type:

  • No text on screen is smaller than 16px (type.minimum); a test checks every stylesheet.
  • Son's lines and the learner's lines are set upright, not in italic.
  • Quotes and apostrophes are curly. The course turns straight quotes into curly ones when it loads the content. An ellipsis ("…") is one character.
  • tokens.json also holds two styles that the course does not use: title (Archivo 700, 88 / 84, -0.03em, 52px on a phone) and thought (IBM Plex Sans italic 400, 18 / 27). The largest type the course shows is display.

Space. tokens.json sets these spacing steps, on a 4px base: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64 and 88px. Inside small parts, the stylesheets also use 6, 10, 14, 18, 22 and 28px. A paper box has 20px of padding at the top and bottom and 22px at the sides, cards have 16 to 24px, and the gaps between items are 8 to 20px.

Radius

Shadow

Layout. A breakpoint is a screen width at which the layout changes.

Focus ring. The focus ring is a 3px lamp outline, 2px outside the control, around a 2px ink ring. The ink ring shows on paper, and the amber outline shows on the dark ground. When a new step moves focus to a heading or a card, that heading or card shows no ring. Radio cards and the member search show the ring around the whole box.

Targets. Every target is at least 44px (--cz-target). Main buttons, choices and call bar buttons are 56px (--cz-target-l), and fields are 48px high (--cz-control-h). Accept and Decline are 72px discs, the controls in a call are 60px, and the days in the date picker are 44px. Every control uses touch-action: manipulation, which stops a quick double tap from zooming the page.

  • The disc is the lamp, and it is amber in the light and dark tones. The amber disc also marks "you are here" on a floor plan, the live dot and the front desk band. Amber is not used for text on light grounds.
  • The smallest sizes are 96px wide for the lockup (25mm in print), 72px wide for the wordmark (18mm) and 16px for the symbol (5mm). On every side, the logo keeps a clear space as wide as the symbol's disc. These are the brand rules of the Cozy design system.
  • In the course, the symbol is 112px as the lamp lands in it, then 56px with the wordmark beside it over the intro cards. It is 28px (dark tone) beside "Front desk training" on the Welcome screen, 72px (dark) as the desk computer starts and its lamp lights, 32px (28px below 760px) in the Cozy Desk header, 24px on the member card and the sign-in screen, and 22px on the case status box. The lockup is 26px high on the survey card.
  • The header and footer of this project's documents use a fifth tone, lit: paper letters and a paper square with the amber lamp disc, so that the logo stands out on the dark header. The course does not use it.

The icons are one set of line drawings on a 24px grid, with a 2px stroke and rounded line ends and corners. Each icon takes the color of the text around it and has no fill, except a rating star and the note icon on a Noted button, which fill when they are on. An icon is decorative, and hidden from screen readers, unless it has a label. Sizes run from 16 to 32px, most often 18 to 22px. Each icon has one meaning.

The set also holds the icons that Cozy's other pieces use, drawn the same way: wifi for Wi-Fi outages (the Wi-Fi Down card and Cozy in 60 seconds), meeting for the meeting rooms on the floor plan in Cozy in 60 seconds, and fridge, cleaning and mug for the kitchen rules in Cozy in 60 seconds (food labels, the Friday clear-out and cups). This project's documents also use notes, thought, forward and headphones.

The faces are Open Peeps, a free set of hand-drawn people by Pablo Stanley, which anyone may use (section 15 gives the license). They were made with DiceBear, an open-source library that builds faces from such sets, in its open-peeps style. Each person and mood has one SVG file, public/faces/<person>-<mood>.svg, 704 × 704.

How faces are shown:

  • Each face is round, on a disc: desk #F4EFE4 by default, and paper in bubbles, in Son's boxes, in the survey comment and on the After the call screen.
  • Face sizes, in px: 32 and 40 (Son's bar, folded and open), 36 and 44 (Son's chat), 44 (member bubbles), 48 (call bar, practice list), 56 (survey comment), 64 (member card, practice call), 72 (the talk with Dan), 124 and 128 (the phone in a call and while it rings), 160 (After the call).
  • The lamp color marks the voice: a 4px lamp ring around Sofia's face while her line plays, and a soft lamp ring 6px out that pulses while her call rings.
  • When the learner declines or ends the call, her face dims to 50%.
  • The mood is a word next to the face: "Sofia · annoyed" over her bubble (the name alone while she is neutral), a mood chip under her name on the phone, the mood under her name on the call bar, and "Omar · tired" in the practice call. Son's name tag reads "Son · Your guide".

Document layer

The written documents of this project share four parts: a reading-progress bar at the top of the page, a section head, a callout (a boxed aside) and a stat strip (a row of numbers, each with its current value and its goal). The parts come in six accent tones: amber, blue, green, red, purple and brown. Each section head has a tone, and the section's number in the contents list repeats it, so the reader can find the section again. Sections next to each other have different tones, so the reader sees where one ends and the next begins. A callout's tone matches what it holds: amber for a note, blue for evidence, purple for a method, brown for background such as the limits of the evidence, and red for a warning.

The stat strip shows a current value and a goal value on one scale, and it fills both bars when it scrolls into view. Numbers that have no goal go in a table.

Three more parts help the reader find their way through a document. The meta row in the document's header shows the reading time, the number of sections and the number of figures. It counts them from the document after the page loads, so nobody has to type or update them. Dense figures have an Enlarge button and open full screen on the dark ground, because a large figure, such as a branch map of the call, does not fit the 760px reading column. The Escape key, the Close button or a click on the dark ground closes them. Long reference lists fold into groups that show how many items each holds. The groups start closed.

Numbered lists have a 30px amber circle around each number, and bulleted lists have a 7px amber dot, so a sequence of steps looks different from a plain list. Subheads have a 6px lamp bar at the left.

Motion is calm and unhurried. One thing moves at a time, and each movement finishes before the next one starts. The values are set in src/motion/presets.js and base.css. Each movement has a duration and an easing curve, which sets how the movement speeds up and slows down; the curves are written as cubic-bezier values.

Morphs. When one thing becomes another, it morphs: it changes its shape and place until it is the new thing, so the eye can follow it. A morph takes 1.1s unless noted. The course has these morphs:

  1. 1
    The lamp disc rises into the lamp of the Cozy symbol (2.2s, slowing as it lands); the square fades in around it (1s, from 1.3s).
  2. 2
    Over the intro cards, the symbol shrinks from 112 to 56px, and "Cozy" from "Welcome to Cozy", drawn as the wordmark, moves in beside it.
  3. 3
    The face-down phone turns over into the incoming call.
  4. 4
    On desktop, the ringing phone moves from the middle to the left as Sofia's member card slides in.
  5. 5
    The incoming call screen becomes the call; the call buttons scale in from 90%.
  6. 6
    The line the learner picks or taps becomes their bubble.
  7. 7
    A noted line flies into Your note, shrinking to 15%.
  8. 8
    At Part 2, the phone settles face down as the desk computer comes in.
  9. 9
    After sign-in, the computer's screen grows into the Cozy Desk window.
  10. 10
    The survey stars fill one by one.

Entrances and exits. Things fade in with a 14px rise and leave with a 7px rise. A bubble grows from its speaker's side, from 92% of its size and 12px to the side, anchored at the speaker's bottom corner. Son's messages grow from the left. Intro cards slide 40px in from the side they move from. A screen fades out before the next fades in, starting at its top. Panels, sheets, the date picker and the member list rise 8px. Son's bar fades one line out before the next. Note lines slide in 12px from the right and leave to the left.

Loops, movements that repeat, run for as long as they are needed.

Reduced motion is a setting on the device that asks apps to move less. The course follows it through Motion, the animation library it uses, by setting reducedMotion to user in MotionConfig. With the setting on, slides, scales, turns and morphs are turned off, so things fade in place. Panels, the date picker and the member list fade in over 250ms, and hover changes are instant. The lamp, its rings, the power button, the voice bars and the computer's lamp stand still; the face-down phone shows one still ring; Accept and End show a still 4px lamp ring (3px on the call bar); Son's button keeps a still 3px glow; typing dots fade without the bounce; and the ring on the starting computer turns once every 3s. A noted line stays in place while the note's count changes, and scrolling moves at once.

Scrolling is smooth. The conversation scrolls to follow new lines while the learner is within 140px of the bottom; when they have scrolled up to read, it stays where they are. When focus moves, the page does not scroll, and pressing a button does not make the page jump. The top bar stays in view, and panels are fixed to the window.

  • The three sounds are original and were made for the course, so that none of them sounds like a brand's ringtone or chime. They were made with ffmpeg, a free tool for making and editing audio, as single-channel MP3 files at 32 kbps (kilobits a second), a small size that is enough for short sounds.
  • Sound starts after the learner's first tap, because browsers block sound until a person interacts with the page. That tap unlocks the course's two players, one for the voice and one for the cues (the three short sounds).
  • Sound is on by default. In the short chat with Son before the shift starts, the learner picks "Sound on" or "Keep it quiet". During the call, the Speaker button turns sound on and off, and "Sound off" shows once on the phone or the call bar.
  • Captions stay on, and the top bar says so with "Captions on". Each caption shows the captions icon and the caption in mono, and it is announced politely.
  • Sofia's lines are spoken, and the lines of Son, the learner, Omar and Dan are text. While her line plays, her bubble shows "Playing" with five amber voice bars, and her face has the lamp ring. With sound on, Replay plays the line again.
  • All 30 of Sofia's lines have a recording. If a recording fails to load, the line shows at reading pace, and the course goes on.
  • Pause and save stops everything, the audio and the call clock included.
  • The course is written in plain US English, in short sentences. Headings, buttons and labels use sentence case, with a capital letter for the first word and for names.
  • The course uses no em dashes. It has no exclamation marks except in "Let's rock!", the main button on the Welcome screen.
  • A middle dot joins a name and its detail: "Part 1 · The call", "Son · Your guide", "Sofia · annoyed", "Job aid · Front desk · 01".
  • Buttons name the action they do: "Sign in", "Call Sofia", "Save entry", "Check my entry", "Close the case", "Print", "Start over", "Keep my progress".
  • Son's button says "Next" when he has more to say. When something waits behind his line, or when nothing does, it says "Got it" or names the action itself ("Show me around", "Her details", "Call her back", "Let's rock!"). His last line before the learner's own move has no button, because the move, such as the choices, comes in with that line.
  • Son's lines are short, warm guidance, set as upright text with no voice. He says what a phone call hides ("Looks like she feels questioned. She's giving you less."). On the learner's first try at a decision, he gives no hint at the answer.
  • Members speak in everyday words, set in Archivo, with one to three short sentences to a bubble ("Yesterday someone was on the phone right next to me for half an hour.").
  • When Cozy speaks to members, on the signs and in Cozy in 60 seconds, it is short, kind and specific, and it gives the alternative next to the rule: "Silent zone. Calls and meetings in Flex →".
  • When Cozy speaks to staff, in Cozy Desk, the job aids and the Wi-Fi Down card, it is direct and goes step by step, with mono labels: "Open cases", "On shift: [name]", "Due today: Call Sofia back (#0418)".
  • States are shown as words: "Right", "Not yet", "Met", "(empty)", "Choose one", "Choose a date", "Tap to fill", "[n] fields need you before saving: [fields]."
  • Dates are in US order, with the month before the day. In speech, a date is the weekday ("by Wednesday"); in Cozy Desk and the note, it is the short date ("Wed, Oct 7"). Times are used in the note, in 24-hour form ("10:16"), while promises and Cozy Desk use days. The course's days are working days, so the quick picks "In 1 day" and "In 2 days" skip weekends.
  • The course uses the same word for the same thing every time: member, community manager, front desk, Cozy Desk, case, owner, to-do, the note, the log, job aids, and the zones Focus, Flex, Private and Kitchen. Sections 1 and 4 explain them.

The job aid cards are built from the PrintableCard and JobAidCard components.

  • In the course, each card has a Print button. It copies the card onto a sheet of its own and opens the browser's print dialog, where the learner can also save a PDF.
  • The card prints alone, on white, centered on the page both ways, 140mm wide, with 12mm page margins and no shadow. Its colors print as they look on screen, because the CSS setting print-color-adjust: exact keeps the browser from dropping background colors.
  • Every card has the same parts. On screen, it has 10px corners and the raise shadow. At the top is the eyebrow in mono ("Job aid · Front desk · 01"), then the title in Archivo 700 at 28px, with a 30px icon at the top right, in the zone's color when the icon is a zone. Each row has a key in amber ink (Archivo 700 at 17px) and text in IBM Plex Sans 400 at 16px (line height 1.35), with a bold lead in 600. An optional foot sits on desk. Across the bottom runs the band: ink, 40px high, with the lamp dot and "Front desk · Ask here" in mono capitals with 0.08em tracking, or a zone band in its place.
  • Cards 01 to 03 come from the course: 01 HEARD card (icon desk, keys H, E, A, R, D), 02 Options card (icon focus in Focus blue, keys 1 to 4) and 03 The log card (icon cases, keys 1 to 5). Card 04 is the Wi-Fi Down card, described below.

The Wi-Fi Down card

  • The Wi-Fi Down card is a one-page READ-DO checklist kept at the front desk. It has the same parts as the other cards: the eyebrow "Job aid · Front desk · 04", the title "Wi-Fi Down", the wifi icon and the front desk band across the bottom.
  • Under the title, a "Use it when" line on desk, with a 4px lamp edge, says when to use the card. The seven steps follow as numbered rows. Each row has its key in amber ink, a bold lead that names the step, its time in mono amber ink with the alarm icon, one action, and an "Avoid:" line in Not yet red with the alert icon. A foot on desk holds the five messages to send, with the times left blank to fill in. The card is wider than cards 01 to 03.
  • When it is printed, it follows the same rules as the other cards, on one page: alone on white, centered, with 12mm margins, exact colors and no shadow. It fills the page's printable width, and its text stays at 16px (12pt) or larger.

The font files hold the Latin letters, including accented ones (the latin and latin-ext subsets). Their license texts are kept beside them in src/design/fonts/.

Sofia's recorded lines, in public/audio/sofia/, are synthetic speech made from the text of each line, and the course's credits screen says so.
