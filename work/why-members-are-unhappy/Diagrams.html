1Three causes and their fixes

Cozy is a business that rents desks and rooms in three coworking spaces, shared workspaces where members work on monthly plans. Each space has three zones: Focus is a silent zone, Flex is for desk work, calls and meetings, and Private has closed rooms. Front-desk staff, whom Cozy calls community managers, welcome members and handle their complaints. Every quarter, Cozy runs a member survey with scores from 1 to 5. Cozy and its data are fictional and were created for this portfolio. The data is based on real public sources: surveys of coworking members, public reviews of coworking spaces and studies of how businesses handle complaints.

Each problem behind a drop in member satisfaction was traced to its cause with the Six Boxes, Carl Binder’s version of Thomas Gilbert’s Behavior Engineering Model. The model lists six things that shape what people do at work. Three are in the environment: the information, tools and incentives people have. Three are in the person: their knowledge, their motives and their capacity, meaning whether they are able to do the work. The environment is checked first, because changing it usually costs less than training. The first two causes are in the environment, so signs, a guide, a checklist and a change of tools fix them. The third cause is in the environment and in the person, so its fix is a change to the log and a course.

2Action map

Hear Them Out is a course of about 20 minutes, built as a branching scenario, in which each choice the learner makes changes what happens next. The learner takes a complaint call from Sofia, a member who is upset about a long phone call that another member, Dan, took next to her in Focus, and then logs the case in Cozy Desk, a practice copy of the software Cozy’s front desk uses. The call follows HEARD, a five-step way of handling complaints taught by the Disney Institute: Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve and Diagnose.

The course was planned with action mapping, a method for planning training created by Cathy Moore. The map starts from the business goal and lists what front-desk staff must do on a complaint call and after it. Each action gets a practice in the course and the minimum information it needs, meaning what staff need to know to do it. That information goes into three job aids, short reference cards: the HEARD card, the Options card and the Log card. A barrier is something at work that keeps staff from doing an action. Where a barrier is in the environment, such as a log that does not ask for a date, Cozy’s managers fix it instead of the course. These barriers are marked “→ Managers”, and their fixes are listed under “Changes for Cozy’s managers”.

3The learner’s path

The course runs from the opening through the call with Sofia to Cozy Desk and three practice activities. Son, a guide character who stays with the learner through the course, explains each step in text and describes Sofia’s mood after each reply. Right after the call, the course branches. When every decision took the best option, Sofia hangs up and waits to see whether Cozy follows up, and the course goes on to a summary and Part 2, where the learner logs the case. On any other path, the call ends and the course jumps ahead a week to Sofia’s answer in the member survey. A debrief then reviews the call, and a rewind goes back to the decision where the call turned.

The three practice activities come after the course and do not count toward passing. In the practice call, an AI plays the member, and a second AI checks the call against three criteria. In the written practice, an AI checks the two text entries that the learner writes. AI scoring can vary, so these checks give feedback without a score. The private talk is a short exercise of five questions.

4The branches of the call

The call runs in real time through five decisions, one for each step of HEARD. Every option goes on to Sofia’s reply and the next line, and a weaker option changes her mood. The decisions are judged on two axes, two parts of the call that are checked separately. The talk axis covers decisions 1 to 3, where the learner listens, shows empathy and apologizes. The resolve axis covers decisions 4 and 5, which decide whether her problem gets solved. One weaker option makes its axis not yet right, and together the two axes set Sofia’s answer in the member survey.

The call ends before the survey. On a weaker path, the course jumps ahead a week to her answer. On the best path, her 5 out of 5 comes after Part 2, once the learner has kept the promise. In the lines below, [call date] and [call time] are the learner’s own date and time, [name] is the learner’s name, and [day 1] and [day 2] are the next two working days after the call.
