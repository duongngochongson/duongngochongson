1Three causes, one skill gap

Each problem behind the drop was traced to its cause with Gilbert’s behavior engineering model, in Binder’s six boxes: the environment first (information, tools, incentives), then the person (knowledge, capacity, motives). Only the third cause reaches the person, so only it needs a course; the other two are fixed in the environment, faster and for less.

2Action map

The map starts from the business goal and lists what front-desk staff must do on a complaint call and after it. Each action gets a practice in the course and the minimum information it needs. Where the barrier is in the environment, the fix goes to the managers instead of into the course.

3The learner’s path

The course runs from the opening through the call to Cozy Desk and the practices. Right after the call it branches: the best path goes on to the summary and Part 2, and any other path meets Sofia’s survey at once, then a debrief and a rewind to the decision where the call turned.

4The branches of the call

The call runs in real time through five decisions, one for each step of HEARD. Every option goes on to Sofia’s reply and the next line; a weaker one changes her mood and costs its axis. Talk takes decisions 1 to 3 and Resolve takes 4 and 5, and the two axes give Sofia’s survey answer a week later.
