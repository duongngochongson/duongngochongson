1Three causes and their solutions

With the Behavior Engineering Model (BEM), I found three root causes and matched each with a solution, and only one of them is a course.

Cozy, a coworking business with three spaces, is a concept project made for this portfolio. Two of the causes sit only in the environment, so signs, a checklist and a tool solve them, and the one about complaints also needs a course.

How I sorted the causes

I sorted each cause into the BEM’s six cells, in Carl Binder’s plain-language version of Gilbert’s model, and checked the environment first, because changing it costs less than training. For each cause, I asked Mager and Pipe’s question: could people do it if they had to? If so, a course would not help.

2Action map

With action mapping, I chose five actions that resolve a complaint, and Hear Them Out practices all five in one call and its follow-up.

Following Moore’s action mapping (2017), each action has a practice and the minimum information it needs, which goes into the job aids. Barriers in the environment, marked “→ Managers”, go to Cozy’s managers instead of the course.

3Learner’s path

The course has two parts: Part 1, the call, with five decisions, and Part 2, which the learner reaches only if all five went right.

Right after the call, the course branches. If all five decisions went right, Sofia waits to see whether Cozy follows up, and the learner moves on to Part 2. If a decision went wrong, the course jumps ahead a week to her survey answer, then a debrief and a rewind. After the course, the learner can practice more if they want, in three ways, and none of them is scored.

4Branches of the call

The call has five decisions, one for each step of HEARD, and together they set Sofia’s score.

HEARD, a service recovery technique taught by the Disney Institute, stands for Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve and Diagnose. Decisions 1 to 3 make up the talk axis and decisions 4 and 5 the resolve axis, and together they set Sofia’s survey answer. [name], [call date] and [call time] are the learner’s own, and [day 1] and [day 2] are the next two working days after the call.
