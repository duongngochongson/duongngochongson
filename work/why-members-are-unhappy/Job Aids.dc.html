A job aid is a short reference card that people use while they work. These four cards are for Cozy's front-desk staff, whom Cozy calls community managers. Staff work in shifts at the front desk, where they welcome members, answer their questions and handle their complaints.

Cards 01 to 03 come from Hear Them Out, a course in which staff handle a complaint call from a member, then log the case and follow it up. In the course, the cards open from a Job aids panel and can be printed, and this page shows them with the same words. The HEARD card sets out HEARD, a five-step way of handling complaints taught by the Disney Institute: Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve and Diagnose. The Options card lists what staff can offer an upset member. It also lists the problems that go to someone else: to the head of operations, who manages the buildings, the internet and the suppliers, or to the incident process, Cozy's separate procedure for serious incidents such as safety problems and harassment. The log card says how to record a case in the log, where staff record complaints, and how to follow the case through to the end. Every week, Cozy's managers review the log to check the follow-ups and to find the causes of complaints.

Card 04, the Wi-Fi Down card, is for internet outages. It is a one-page checklist kept at the front desk, of the kind called READ-DO: during an outage, staff read each step and do it before going on to the next. Each step says when to do it and names the mistake to avoid, and the card ends with the messages to send to members. All four cards share one layout, so the Wi-Fi Down card reads like the three cards staff know from the course: the card number and title at the top, then the steps in rows, each marked with a number or a letter.

Why the Wi-Fi Down card works this way

These reasons are not printed on the card.

  • Job aids suit tasks that are rare, complex or costly to get wrong (Rossett and Schafer, 2007). Staff could do each step of an outage if they had to, but outages are rare and stressful, and each shift took the steps in a different order. So the solution is a checklist at the front desk, which keeps the steps in the same order on every shift.
  • A wait with no explanation and no known end feels longer than one with a promised update (Maister, 1985). So the first notice goes out within 10 minutes, before the cause is known, and every message says when the next update will come.
  • Atlassian, a software company, publishes guidance for teams that report outages of online services. It says to tell users early, update them at a set pace and take responsibility for the outage, including one caused by a supplier (Atlassian Statuspage). On the card, an update goes out every 15 minutes, and the first notice says sorry for the trouble.
  • Before the card, each shift decided on its own what to give members to make up for an outage. On the card, everyone present is offered a drink at the first notice, and after an outage of 30 minutes or more, everyone present also gets a free hour in a room. The rule is the same on every shift, because people judge a procedure fair when it is applied the same way each time (Leventhal, 1980). The free hour gives back what members lost, which is working time (Roschk and Gelbrich, 2014).
