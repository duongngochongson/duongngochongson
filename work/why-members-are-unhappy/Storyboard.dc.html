• [name] is the learner's first name. [call date] and [call time] are when the learner starts the note, before answering the call. [day 1] is the next working day after the call (working days are Monday to Friday), and [day 2] is the working day after [day 1]. [day 2 after day 1] is [day 2] as the learner's promise at decision 4 says it. Usually it reads the same as [day 2]. When a weekend comes between [day 1] and [day 2], as after a call on a Thursday, it reads "[date], the next working day after that", with [day 2] in place of [date]. All of these dates are worked out once, from the real date on which the learner takes the course, and saved with the learner's progress.
  • Dates and times are in US order. In what people say, a date is a weekday ("by Wednesday"). In Cozy Desk and in the note, it is the short date ("Wed, Oct 7"). Promises and Cozy Desk give days without times. Times appear in the notes, in 24-hour form ("10:16").
  • Voice: Sofia's lines have audio, and the learner's lines and Son's lines are text with no audio. Her text on screen can include [name] or a date, but her audio leaves them out, so the same audio works for every learner. Where the two differ, this storyboard gives both.
  • Decisions: the call has five decisions, one for each step of HEARD, a five-step way of handling complaints taught by the Disney Institute: Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve and Diagnose. Decisions 1 to 4 have three options each, and decision 5 has two, shown in random order. Each weaker option has one clear mistake. The debrief, a review of the call after the ending, names that mistake. In the tables, the best option is listed first.
  • Axes: Sofia's score in the member survey at the end depends on two axes, the two things she judges. The talk axis is decisions 1 to 3: whether the learner listened, showed empathy and apologized well. The resolve axis is decisions 4 and 5: whether the fix and the case status solved her problem. Each decision below names its axis.
  • Flags: two weaker choices set a flag, a marker that the course remembers for later. Naming the other member at decision 3 sets name-shared, which adds a line to the ending. Giving no date at decision 4 sets no-date, which leaves the case without a follow-up date.
  • Tap-to-say lines are moments when the learner has one line to say and taps it to say it. They keep the learner speaking between decisions and show an example of a good move, such as "Go on, I'm listening."
  • Moods are the names of Sofia's faces, the drawings that show how she feels: annoyed, telling, calmer, hurt, doubtful, let-down, set-on-acting, satisfied, neutral, serious. In the call, the mood also shows as a word next to her face, except when she is neutral.
  • Lead lines are what Sofia says before a decision or a tap-to-say line. Each lead line has two versions. The warm version follows the best choice at the decision before. The guarded version follows a weaker choice, or a call back after the learner hung up on her. Both versions give the facts the next step needs.
  • Son, the guide: Son is a guide character who stays with the learner through the whole course. His name tag reads "Son · Your guide". Every piece of guidance in the course is his: the welcome, the cards, the setup, the tips and the moments between screens. After each decision, he describes Sofia's mood in a line of text ("Looks like Sofia's hurt. That sounded like blame."), because on a phone call the learner cannot see her face. Sofia cannot hear him. He gives no hint before the learner's first try at a decision, so each first choice is the learner's own. He has no voice. His face, like the members' faces, comes from Open Peeps, a free library of hand-drawn people: a young Asian man with short dark hair and glasses, friendly and slightly techie. It has four moods: smiling, thinking, concerned (a gentle worry) and pleased.
  • Where Son speaks: in the opening, before the phone turns over, Son speaks in chat bubbles. From the incoming call on, he speaks from Son's bar at the top of the screen. The bar sits apart from the conversation, so his words do not mix with Sofia's (see The call screen). On the results, his bar sits under the two questions. Son takes no part in two of the practices after the course: the call with Omar, another member, and the private talk with Dan, the member who made the call next to Sofia.
  • Son's button: each of his messages has at most one button, so his lines come one at a time, when the learner is ready. It is Next (an arrow) when he has more to say, or the action itself when his line leads somewhere, such as Show me around, Her details or Call her back. Got it (a tick) folds his bar until he speaks again, and brings in what waits behind his line, such as the case status after the call back. To fold the bar means to shrink it to a slim strip. His last line before the learner's own move has no button: it stays open while the move comes in with it (the line to note, a line to tap, the choices, End, Accept, or the screen's own button), and his bar folds once the move is made. While it waits, the button breathes softly with the lamp's glow; no line tells the learner to tap it.
  • Son's look: his messages are a dark box on the dark ground, with a 1.5px dashed outline in the warm gray the brand uses for lines. The box holds his face, his name tag in a monospaced font and his line in the brightest light color, upright and a little heavier than body text. His button sits level with his line, or under it when the box is too narrow. The dark box sets him apart from Sofia's paper bubbles.
  • Bubbles: Sofia's longer turns show as separate bubbles. Her lines come in one by one, as their audio plays, or at a brisk reading pace when sound is off. Show the whole turn shows the rest of the turn at once, and Replay (with sound on) plays a line again.
  • Turn order: in the call, a turn is always Sofia's lines, then Son's, then the learner's move (the choices, a line to tap and say, or End). After a decision, the order is the learner's bubble, Sofia's reply and her next lead line, then Son's line about her mood. Nothing overlaps: the move comes in with Son's last line, which stays open, or, when Son has nothing to say, once Sofia has finished.
  • The note is the learner's own note of the call. Every line in the call, Sofia's and the learner's, can go into it.
  • Choices cannot be changed during the call. After the ending, the rewind takes the learner back to the first decision that went wrong.

  • Pace: calm and unhurried. One thing moves at a time, and each movement finishes before the next one starts, with a short rest between them.
  • Things come in over about 0.8 to 1 second and slow down as they arrive. They leave over about half a second, and items in a group follow each other about 150ms apart. A morph takes 1 to 1.2 seconds, so the eye can follow it.
  • When one thing becomes another, it morphs, which means it changes shape and moves smoothly into the new thing:
    • the lamp disc becomes the lamp in the Cozy symbol, the picture part of the logo
    • the Cozy wordmark (the name Cozy in the logo's lettering) in "Welcome to Cozy" moves to its place beside the symbol, as in the Cozy lockup (the full logo, with the symbol and the wordmark side by side), as the five cards come in
    • the face-down phone turns over into the incoming call
    • on desktop, the phone moves from the middle to the left as Sofia's member card comes in
    • the line the learner picks becomes their bubble
    • a noted line flies into the note
    • the incoming call screen becomes the call
    • the phone going face down gives way to the desk computer
    • after sign-in, the computer's screen grows into the Cozy Desk window
    • the survey stars fill one by one
  • When nothing morphs, things fade in with a short slide (12 to 16px).
  • A new bubble grows from its speaker's side. Before Sofia's bubbles and Son's messages, three typing dots show for about a second.
  • Sofia's lines move on by themselves, at the pace of her voice, while each of Son's lines waits for his button.
  • Scrolling is smooth. While the learner is at the bottom of the conversation, it scrolls to follow new lines. When the learner has scrolled up, it stays where it is. The page never jumps to the top when the learner presses a button; a new screen starts at its top.
  • Loops (the lamp's breath, the ring, the pulse on Accept and on End, the breath of Son's button and of the computer's power button) run for as long as they are needed.

How this course works

Five cards come in one at a time. Each has an icon, a title and short points with the key words in bold. Under each card, ‹ and › sit on both sides of "[n] of 5": ‹ goes back a card (not on the first), and › goes on. On the last card, › becomes the round Got it tick, which leads to the Welcome.

  1. 1
    About this course (icon: info)
    • A simulation of a shift on Cozy's front desk. You play the community manager: you take a call from an upset member, then log it and follow it up.
    • Members who complain want two things: to feel heard, and a fix they can count on, on time.
    • You'll practice both: listening until the member feels heard, solving the right problem, and logging it so it gets done.
    • The goal for Cozy: members rate how we handle complaints higher.
  2. 2
    Cozy in 10 seconds (with a small floor plan of the zones and "You · on the front desk")
    • Focus is for silent work. No calls.
    • Flex is for desk work, calls and meetings.
    • Private has rooms for members on a dedicated plan.
    • You're on the front desk.
  3. 3
    Part 1 · The call (icon: phone)
    • A member calls the front desk, upset. At each moment, you pick what to say.
    • Aim for the member to feel heard, not just answered. A quick fix on its own won't land.
    • After each choice, I'll tell you how they're feeling.
    • Mistakes are part of it. You'll see how it lands, then try again from where it turned.
  4. 4
    Your note (icon: note)
    • Tap Note on any line worth keeping: who called, what happened, what they need, and anything you promise.
    • You'll need your note in Part 2. The log only knows what you wrote down.
  5. 5
    Part 2 · Cozy Desk (icon: monitor)
    • A practice copy of Cozy Desk, the front desk software.
    • You log the case from your note, set the next step, and follow up on the day you promised.
    • About 20 minutes in all. Your place is saved, and you can restart any time.

HEARD card

  • Hear: Let them tell it ("What happened? Take me through it."), and let them finish ("Go on, I'm listening.").
  • Empathize: Name the feeling ("That sounds exhausting"), and say their need back in your own words ("So you need somewhere you can rely on"), before any rule or fix.
  • Apologize: Say exactly what Cozy got wrong ("We missed your message, and that's on us"). Names stay private on both sides ("I can't share names, the same way I'd never share yours").
  • Resolve: Offer an option that fits the need. Agree on one next step with an owner and a day.
  • Diagnose: Keep the case open until the member says it worked. Log what happened, where and when; the weekly review uses the log to find the cause, not only to fix this case.

Options card

  • A Focus desk, with the zone map: fits when the member needs reliable quiet and sits outside Focus.
  • A private talk with the other member, keeping the reporter's name out: fits when the other member broke a zone rule.
  • Pausing the plan from the end of the month: fits when the member needs a break.

The storyboard had a cognitive walkthrough, a review in which the reviewer goes through each moment as a learner and asks whether the learner would know what to do next. It was done twice: once as Jane and once as a new hire. Jane is a persona, a made-up but typical learner, based on the shift interviews and the issue log. She has three years on the Cozy front desk and trusts her habits. The new hire knows nothing about Cozy or the course. The table shows what each of them would do at nine moments, and how the storyboard helps them.

Sofia's lines are synthetic speech, made with a text-to-speech voice. Each of her lines has a line id, a short name such as d1-a (decision 1, option a). Its recording is an MP3 file named after that id, in the course's audio folder (public/audio/sofia). All 30 recordings exist. If a recording fails to load, its line shows at reading pace instead, so the call goes on.

Son's lines and the learner's lines have no audio, and neither do the practices: Omar's practice call, the talk with Dan and the written practice are text.
