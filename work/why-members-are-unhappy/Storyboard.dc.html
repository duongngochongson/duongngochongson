• [name] is the learner's first name. [call date] and [call time] are when the learner starts the note. [day 1] is the next working day after the call, and [day 2] the working day after that.
  • Sofia's lines are spoken and shown on screen. The learner's lines and Son's lines are text.
  • Decisions 1 to 4 have three options and decision 5 has two, shown in random order. The tables list the best option first. Each weaker option has one mistake, which the debrief names.
  • Decisions 1 to 3 are on the talk axis, and decisions 4 and 5 on the resolve axis. Together they set Sofia's survey answer.
  • Lead lines, what Sofia says before the learner's move, come in two versions: warm after a best choice, and guarded after a weaker one.
  • Moods are the names of Sofia's faces. After each decision, Son describes her mood, because the learner cannot see her. He never hints before a first try.
  • Two weaker choices set a flag: naming Dan sets name-shared, and giving no date sets no-date.

The opening

The learner turns on a lamp, which rises into the Cozy symbol. Son introduces himself: "Hi, welcome to Cozy. I'm Son." Five cards follow, one at a time:

  • About this course. "A simulation of a shift on Cozy's front desk. You play the community manager: you take a call from an upset member, then log it and follow it up." "Members who complain want two things: to feel heard, and a fix they can count on, on time."
  • Cozy in 10 seconds. "Focus is for silent work. No calls." "Flex is for desk work, calls and meetings." "Private has rooms for members on a dedicated plan."
  • Part 1 · The call. "Aim for the member to feel heard, not just answered. A quick fix on its own won't land." "Mistakes are part of it. You'll see how it lands, then try again from where it turned."
  • Your note. "Tap Note on any line worth keeping: who called, what happened, what they need, and anything you promise." "You'll need your note in Part 2. The log only knows what you wrote down."
  • Part 2 · Cozy Desk. "You log the case from your note, set the next step, and follow up on the day you promised."

The learner then signs in with a first name and chooses Sound on or Keep it quiet. Son: "All set. Your shift starts now."

Decision 5 · Diagnose

Resolve axis. After the call, Cozy Desk opens a box: "Sofia · Long call next to her in Focus · Set the status." The prompt is What status does the case get?

On the best path, the screen says: "Sofia hangs up. She feels heard, and she's waiting to see what you do." Son: "She's counting on [day 2]. Now let's make sure it happens." Part 2 follows.

On any other path, Son says: "Let's jump ahead a week and see how it went." Sofia's member survey card shows her answer to "How did Cozy handle your complaint?"

The card adds one line on what happened that week, from the first resolve choice that went wrong:

  • All right: "On [day 2] you called her back. Her mornings in Focus have been quiet."
  • Decision 4, no date: "Nobody called her back, and she never heard what happened."
  • Decision 4, the reminder: "The reminder went to everyone, and the calls in Focus went on."
  • Decision 5, closed: "The case dropped off the list. Nobody talked to the member, and nobody called her back."

When talk is not yet, the line adds: "The call itself left her feeling unheard." When name-shared is set, the ending adds: "A few days later, Dan asks the front desk who reported him."

"Your call, step by step" lists the five decisions, each marked Right or Not yet, with an icon and the word. Son: "Let's look at where it turned."

  • The first decision marked Not yet is open, with its mistake, its debrief and the button "Rewind to [decision name]".
  • The rewind goes back to that moment and keeps the right choices before it. Son: "Back to that moment. Take it from here."
  • From the first ending on, the HEARD and Options cards are in Job aids. After a second miss on the same decision, the debrief points to the card line, such as "See the HEARD card: Apologize."

On the best path, "How you handled the call" shows each step of HEARD next to the line the learner chose, then the learner's note. Son: "HEARD is a way to handle any complaint, one step at a time."

Log screen (Cozy Desk)

The form "New log entry · #0418" sits beside the learner's note. Every field is required, and the first save counts for the score.

The tour. The first time, Son lights one part at a time: "This is Cozy Desk, where every case lives. I'll show you around, one part at a time." He ends: "Fill it in from your note, then save. Your first save is the one that counts." Skip the tour ends it, and Show me around again replays it.

The two text fields are filled in by picking one of two entries:

  • What happened: "Yesterday morning a member took a half-hour call next to Sofia's window desk in Focus. She needs quiet mornings in Focus for her deep work." (right), or "Sofia called to complain again. She was upset, so I listened." (wrong)
  • Next step: "Talk to the member who made the call, in private and without Sofia's name. Point them to Flex for calls." (right), or "Wait and see if it happens again." (wrong)

After a save, each field marked Not yet gives its reason:

  • Member: "The call was from Sofia. Your note starts with her name."
  • Contacted: "The call came in on [call date]. It's the first line of your note."
  • Issue type: "It's a complaint about calls in a zone."
  • Zone or area: "The call was next to her desk in Focus, and only there."
  • What happened: "The entry to pick says what happened, where and when, and what she needs."
  • Next step: "The entry to pick is one concrete action the next shift could do, with no vague words."
  • Owner: "You made the promise, so the case is yours."
  • To-do, the talk: "You told Sofia the talk would be done by [day 1], before the call back."
  • To-do, the call back: "You told Sofia you'd call her on [day 2]. The log has to say what you told her."

If the note is missing a key line, Son says: "Looks like a few things didn't make it into your note." and shows the lines it missed, then: "Next time, note anything she tells you and anything you promise." When every field is right: "Saved. The case is on the open cases list."

A new call (AI)

The learner types up to four messages to Omar, a hot desk member, played by an AI model. Omar's opening: "Hi, it's Omar. I work in Flex. People are on calls around me all morning, and I can't think." Calls are allowed in Flex, so nobody broke a rule: he needs quiet mornings, and nobody showed him that his plan includes Focus.

Before each message, Son gives a hint for the next step of HEARD, such as "Start with Hear: let Omar tell it in his own words. Ask, and don't jump to a rule or a fix." A second model then gives Met or Not yet for each criterion, with a quote and a tip, and shows the model call:

  • Hear: You ask at least one open question and find out when it bothers him and what he needs, before you offer a fix.
  • Empathize and apologize: You acknowledge how he feels, say sorry for something specific Cozy got wrong (nobody showed him the zone map), and keep everyone's name private.
  • Resolve: You offer an option that fits his need (a Focus desk, with the zone map) and agree on a follow-up date.

The model call.

  • Omar: "Hi, it's Omar. I work in Flex. People are on calls around me all morning, and I can't think."
  • You: "That sounds exhausting when you're trying to think. When is it worst, and what would help?"
  • Omar: "Mornings. That's my deep work. I just need somewhere quiet I can count on. And who's the guy at the corner desk? He's on the phone all morning."
  • You: "I can't share names, the same way I'd never share yours. Calls are allowed in Flex, but your plan includes Focus, which is silent. I'm sorry nobody showed you the zone map when you joined. There's a free Focus desk by the window, away from Flex."
  • Omar: "I didn't know I could sit there. Can you show me?"
  • You: "I'll send you the zone map now, and I'll check in on [day 2] to see how your mornings are going."

Write it up (AI)

The learner logs a note about Marta, whose labeled lunch was thrown out at the fridge clean-up on [practice day], a Friday, and types the two text fields. A grader checks each one against two criteria:

  • What happened: "Says what happened, where and when." · "Says what the member needs."
  • Next step: "One concrete action the next shift could do without asking." · "No vague words; the dates go in the to-do list."

The model entries: "On [practice day] around 18:00, Marta's lunch, labeled with her name and the date, was thrown out at the fridge clean-up. She needs labeled food left alone, and to know it won't happen again." and "Ask the cleaning team why labeled food was thrown out at the Friday clean-up, and confirm they keep anything labeled."

Neither AI practice is scored, because a model's feedback can vary. If the service cannot be reached, the model call or the model entries show instead.
