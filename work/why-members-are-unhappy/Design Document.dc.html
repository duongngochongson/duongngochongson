Members give Cozy 2.7 out of 5 for how it handles complaints, the lowest score in the member survey. When an upset member calls, community managers often explain the rules before they ask what happened. They say sorry in general terms and end with "we'll look into it", with no options and no date. About half of the complaints in the front-desk log have a next step, and some members have waited weeks for a reply.

This document sets the shape of the course and the reasons for it: the scenario, the goal, the learner, the action map, the objectives and their alignment, the interactions, the evaluation plan, the design decisions, the job aids and the exercise after the course. Hear Them Out Storyboard v3 holds every line and screen.

The learner is the community manager on shift at the Cozy front desk. The phone rings: Sofia, a member, is upset about another member's long call next to her in Focus, the silent zone. Part 1 is that call, on a phone screen, in real time: five decisions, one per step of HEARD, with a few lines the learner taps to say. Sofia answers in her voice, and her mood shifts. The learner keeps a note as they go. In Part 2 the learner turns to the desk computer, logs the case in Cozy Desk from that note, and follows it through to the call back. Two practices after the course add other cases: a call about calls in Flex, and a log entry about the kitchen.

Son, the learner's guide, is there from the first screen to the last. He explains how things work, has the learner start the note before answering, and after each decision reads Sofia's mood out loud, so the learner sees what a phone call hides. Before the phone turns over, he talks in chat messages; from then on, he speaks from a bar at the top of the screen, apart from the call, one line at a time, when the learner taps his button.

The course has one message, said early and shown often: a member who complains needs to feel heard, and needs a fix they can count on, on time. A quick fix on its own does not land.

The practice case. Sofia, a hot desk member, has been with Cozy for eight months and usually works at the window desk in Focus. Today she calls from home: "I'm working from home today so I can actually get something done." Yesterday morning, while she was preparing a client presentation, another member took a half-hour call right next to her. Two weeks ago she left a note at the front desk about calls in Focus. It went into the log with no owner and no follow-up, so nobody replied and nobody spoke to the member who made the calls. Now she wants to know who made the call.

Key facts:

  • What the member needs: quiet mornings they can count on for focused work.
  • The member who made the call: Dan, a new member, who thought quiet calls were fine in Focus.
  • What Cozy got wrong: her first report had no owner or follow-up, so nobody spoke to Dan, and it happened again. That is exactly what Part 2 practices: an owner for the case, and a day for every promise.

The scenario covers four types of practice:

The course has two parts:

  • Part 1: the call. The five steps of HEARD: Hear, Empathize, Apologize and Resolve during the call, and Diagnose right after it, when the learner sets the case status.
  • Part 2: after the call. How to use the new follow-up process that managers set up: log the case with an owner and a day for every promise, so the open cases list hands it over at shift change, and call the member back on that day.

Other issues are handled elsewhere. Wi-Fi outages follow the Wi-Fi Down card. Money questions (discounts, refunds, billing) go to the head of operations. Safety and harassment follow the incident process. The private talk with the member who made the call is a short exercise after the course (section 11).

Within the next two quarters, raise members' rating of how Cozy handles their complaints from 2.7 to 3.8 out of 5. This course goal is part of Cozy's business goal for the same period: raise overall member satisfaction from 3.2 to 4.0. Two other pieces work toward the same goal: Cozy in 60 seconds, a short guide for new members with the zone map, the house rules and the signs they will see in the space, and the Wi-Fi Down card for staff.

The course aims at the complaint score because a better call changes it directly. Whether a member stays or leaves depends on more than one call.

The learners are Cozy's community managers. They work at the front desks in shifts. Complaints come in between welcome tours, member events, questions and plan changes. They can study for 20 to 30 minutes per shift when it is quiet, on the front-desk computer or their phone.

Jane is a typical community manager taking the course. She is based on the business profile and on the needs analysis (shift interviews and the issue log).

Jane, community manager

  • Experience: three years at Cozy. She knows most members by name.
  • Work: busy day shifts with tours, events and plan changes. She wants calls to be short.
  • Learns on: the front-desk computer in the quiet hour after lunch, or her phone. A call or a member at the desk can interrupt her.
  • Common mistakes: she explains the Focus rule before asking what happened. She names the member who made the call, because she knows everyone: "That must be Dan, he's new." She offers a quick fix that does not match the need, like earplugs or a free coffee. She hands over open cases by word of mouth.
  • Stops learning when: the course tells her what she already knows, or she loses her place after an interruption.

What Jane's profile changes in the design:

Five actions lead to the goal: three during the call (Part 1) and two after it (Part 2). They are based on the steps in the handling research. Barriers are sorted into Cathy Moore's four types: environment, knowledge, skill and motivation. The ones found here are environment, knowledge and skill. The course fixes knowledge and skill barriers. Environment barriers need changes to the process, so they go to managers, following Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model. They are marked "→ Ops" in the table and listed below it.

The call brings the actions together: five decisions, one per step of HEARD, on two axes (talk and resolve), with four survey answers. The learner's note carries the call into the log, and Part 2 follows the case through to the call back. After the course, Practice adds a new call in the learner's own words, played by an AI, the private talk with Dan, and a log entry to write, checked by an AI.

Changes for managers (→ Ops). Managers make these changes in the first quarter. The course teaches the new process, so both start at the same time.

One terminal objective (TO) covers the call, the note and the log. Five enabling objectives (EO1 to EO5) build it, one per step of HEARD, so each scored item belongs to one step. Each objective uses a verb from the revised Bloom's Taxonomy.

  • TO: During a simulated phone call from a member who is upset about another member's call in Focus, the community manager will apply HEARD to handle the call, keep a note of it, and then log it in Cozy Desk from that note, reaching the 5/5 survey answer and saving a log entry with every scored field right.
  • EO1 (Hear): Given an upset member's opening on a call, the community manager will choose the reply that lets the member tell the whole story, before any closed question or rule, and pick the log entry that says what happened and what the member needs.
  • EO2 (Empathize): Given the member's account, the community manager will choose the reply that names her feeling and says her need back, before any rule or fix.
  • EO3 (Apologize): Given a member whose earlier note got no follow-up and who asks who made the call, the community manager will choose the apology that says what Cozy got wrong and keeps names private on both sides.
  • EO4 (Resolve): Given the member's need and Cozy's options, the community manager will select the option that fits the need, with an owner and a day, and enter the next step, the owner and a dated to-do for each promise in the log.
  • EO5 (Diagnose): Given a case at the end of the call, the community manager will determine its status, keeping it open until the member says the fix worked, and identify it in the log by member, contact date, issue type and zone, so the weekly review can find the cause.

Each objective has scored items in the call or the log, and each item also gives practice and feedback. The interaction follows the kind of capability the objective asks for (Gagné): rules are applied in decisions with consequences, and the procedure is done in a simulated tool, from the learner's own note. Every scored item is Right or Not yet on its first try, and the results screen shows the first-try count. Passing asks for mastery instead: the best path through the call and a log saved right, reached through the rewind and the log's checks.

The lines the learner taps to say, the to-do tasks they tap to fill in, and the note itself are not scored. In the course, the two text fields of the log are entries to pick, two for each field: one meets both of the field's criteria, and one is plainly wrong. Writing them is practiced in Practice 3, where an AI checks the learner's own words against the same criteria.

The course is one phone call and one log, with short screens around them. The full text, options and feedback are in Hear Them Out Storyboard v3.

The learner's path.

  1. 1
    The opening. A lamp breathes on a dark screen. Turned on, it rises into the lamp of the Cozy symbol, and "Welcome to Cozy" slides down. Son introduces himself and walks through five cards: About this course (why it matters), Cozy in 10 seconds (the zones), Part 1 · The call, Your note, and Part 2 · Cozy Desk. Then the Welcome, and a short chat with Son to sign in with a first name and choose sound on or off.
  2. 2
    Incoming call. Something rings on the desk: the phone, face down. Turned over, it shows Sofia's incoming call. Son brings in her member card (Her details): on desktop, the phone moves to the left as the card comes in; on a phone, the card opens as a sheet. The card shows her note from two weeks ago, still open, with no owner and no to-do. Before answering, Son has the learner start the note: who's calling and when. The phone stays quiet until the note is started; then it rings, and Accept and Decline come in.
  3. 3
    The call (Part 1). Hear, Empathize, Apologize and Resolve, then the goodbye. The learner ends the call, and Diagnose comes right after it: the case status, in a Cozy Desk box.
  4. 4
    Right after the call. On the best path, Sofia hangs up feeling heard and waiting for the fix. Log the case leads to How you handled the call, a look back at each step of HEARD and the learner's note, and Continue to the computer leads to Part 2. On any other path, the member survey a week later comes at once, then a debrief and a rewind to where it turned.
  5. 5
    Part 2. The desk computer starts with the Cozy symbol, and the learner signs in. In Cozy Desk, Son gives a short tour the first time, and the learner finds the member and logs the case from the note. Then the fast forward: the case on the open cases list at shift change, the talk with the member who made the call, the call back to Sofia on the day promised, and the case closed.
  6. 6
    After Part 2. The member survey a week later: 5/5.
  7. 7
    Results and Practice. The first-try count, the steps to practice, two questions with a next step from Son, the job aids to print, three practices, and a restart.

The call.

  • Layout: on desktop, the phone alone on the left and the conversation on the right, with Son's bar at the top right, under Pause and save. On a phone, a call bar at the top with Son's bar under it.
  • Order: Sofia's lines, then Son's, then the learner's move, so nothing on screen competes. Each of Son's lines waits for his button, and his last line before the move stays open while the move comes in with it.
  • The note: one list in the order taken, like a paper note. Any line in the call, Sofia's or the learner's, can go into it. Its first line, "Sofia · [call date] · [call time]", goes in while the phone rings, before Accept.
  • End: works all through the call. The first time, the line drops (before the greeting, and Sofia calls again) or Sofia is cut off, and the learner calls her back and says sorry. The second time, the call is over, and the learner can start it again. At the goodbye, End is the expected move.
  • Time: real time. The call timer runs from Accept and pauses while the course is paused. The dates in the lines are worked out from the day of the call, in working days, and saved with the progress. When a weekend comes between [day 1] and [day 2], decision 4's promise names the next working day, for example "call you Monday, the next working day after that". A rewind goes on from the call time at that moment.

The branch map.

Sofia's survey answer, a week after the call. Talk is right when decisions 1 to 3 all take the best option, and resolve is right when decisions 4 and 5 both do.

Two hang-ups give 1/5: "They hung up on me, and nobody called back." Sharing the name adds: "A few days later, Dan asks the front desk who reported him."

Part 2.

  • Cozy Desk works like the real software, for any case: twelve issue types, and the areas follow the issue type, with more than one allowed. Dates and the owner are the learner's to pick. The owner list has five first names, the learner's and four teammates'. Each date field has four quick picks: Today, Yesterday, In 1 day and In 2 days, the last two counting working days. Every promise goes in a to-do list with its day.
  • A tour the first time: Son shows Cozy Desk one part at a time, with the rest of the screen dimmed and the part he talks about lit: the member search, the note, Contacted, Issue type and Zone or area, the two entries, Owner, the to-do list and Save entry. Cozy Desk is only shown during the tour, and Next, or a tap anywhere, goes on. Both rows of the to-do list are there from the start, and Son names the two promises they are for and says how the quick picks count. Skip the tour ends it, and Show me around again replays it.
  • The two text fields (What happened, Next step) are entries to pick in the course: two each, one right and one plainly wrong. Writing them is the third practice.
  • The first save comes from the note alone. When a field is wrong, Son shows which key lines the note was missing and reminds the learner to note what they're told and what they promise.
  • The fast forward: at shift change, the open cases list shows Sofia's case with its owner and to-dos, and Son says the evening shift has everything. On [day 1], the talk with the member who made the call. On [day 2], the reminder and the call back: Sofia says the fix worked, and the case status has one option, Close the case.

The screens.

The module measures Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2. Levels 3 and 4 use the issue log and the member survey after launch. In LTEM terms (Thalheimer), the five decisions test decisions in a realistic case (Tier 5), and the log is a realistic task in a simulated tool, with its fields checked by rule (Tier 6). The AI practices cover the call and the written entries in the learner's own words (Tier 6, not scored), and the two questions at the end show how learners see their own readiness (Tier 3).

This portfolio build runs without an LMS: the results and the answers stay in the learner's browser. In a rollout, the same data goes to the LMS: one interaction per scored item with its HEARD step, the first-try score, the pass, and the two answers.

The first-try results show which step of HEARD learners find hardest, so the weekly review and any change to the course start there.

The two questions:

The three cards hold the minimum information from the action map. HEARD and Options open after the first ending of the call, and the log card joins them in Part 2. From then on, they stay in the Job aids panel, and each one prints, or saves as a PDF from the print dialog.

The log entry in Cozy Desk, with the practice case as an example. [day 1] is the next working day after the call, and [day 2] the working day after [day 1].

The talk is right on any day from the call to the day before [day 2], and the call back only on [day 2]. The case stays open until the member says the fix worked. Its status is set right after the call (decision 5) and shows on the open cases list.

A short exercise of five multiple-choice questions, outside the scored course. It follows the practice case: the community manager now talks to Dan, the member who made the call. A pass means choosing the right approach: talk privately, keep the reporter's name out, name the behavior and the alternative, let Dan keep face, and still have the talk.

Q1. When and where do you talk to Dan?

Q2. Dan asks: "Who complained?"

Q3. How do you describe the problem?

Q4. Dan says: "Sorry, I thought quiet calls were fine in Focus."

Q5. The talk went well. What do you do next?
