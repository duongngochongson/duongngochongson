1Action map

Not-Home Delivery Stop is a short online course, or module, of seven slides for delivery drivers at a parcel company. This is a concept project: the company and its numbers were created for this portfolio, so the numbers are targets, not measured results. A not-home stop is a delivery where the driver rings, knocks and waits 60 seconds, and nobody answers. First-attempt success means the parcel reaches an allowed place on that first visit and does not go back to the depot. A missing-parcel claim is a customer’s report that a parcel went missing.

When nobody answers, the parcel can go to a safe place at the house, to a neighbor, or to a pickup point, where the customer collects it later. Each parcel label carries a handling code that sets which of these are allowed. An STD parcel can go to a neighbor or a pickup point, and to a safe place if the stop note or the customer allows it. A SIG parcel needs a signature, so it goes to a neighbor who signs, with the customer’s approval, or to a pickup point. An AGE parcel is age-restricted, so it goes to a pickup point, where the customer shows photo ID. A stop note holds the customer’s delivery instructions.

The module was planned with action mapping, a method for planning training created by Cathy Moore. The map starts from the business goal and lists what drivers must do at a not-home stop. Each action has its barriers, meaning what keeps drivers from doing it, a practice in the module, and the minimum information it needs, which goes into a job aid, a short reference card. Two barriers come from the way the work is set up, so operations, the part of the company that runs the deliveries, solves them instead of the module. They are marked “→ Ops”. One concerns the stop rate, a number on the driver scorecard that tracks each driver’s work. The other concerns the arrival screen, which drivers see when they arrive at a stop.

2Path through the module

The module has seven slides in a row. Slide 2 opens the four job aids, and each of the other six slides practices one or two of the actions in the action map. The scored slides add up to 100 points, and slide 5 is not scored. A driver passes with a score of 80/100 or more if no stop on slide 7 ends with a broken rule, which the module calls a violation. The results screen shows the score against this pass rule, and Try again reopens the slides that lost points.

The module also comes as a SCORM 1.2 package. SCORM is a standard format for a learning management system (LMS), the software companies use to give courses to staff. With it, the LMS runs the module and keeps each driver’s progress and best score.

3Two branching stops

Slide 7 is a branching scenario, in which each choice changes what happens next. It puts the driver at two doors, first with an STD parcel and then with a SIG parcel, and asks for three decisions at each. The best choice earns points and leads to the next decision. The other choices end the stop as returned, when the parcel goes back to the depot, or as a violation, when a rule is broken, or they go on with no points. Every ending of the first stop leads on to the second.

4Typed call on slide 5

On slide 5 the driver calls Mr. Hayes, a customer, about an AGE parcel and types what they say. An artificial intelligence model plays Mr. Hayes, and a second model checks the whole call against the three call moves on the Call moves card. The second model sees the call and the call moves, but not the instructions given to the first. A model’s feedback can vary, so the slide does not count toward the score. If the service cannot be reached, the slide switches to a self-check, so the driver can always finish it.
