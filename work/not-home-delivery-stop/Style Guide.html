1Purpose and scope

This guide sets one visual and interaction system for Not-Home Delivery Stop, a seven-slide eLearning module for delivery drivers about a stop where nobody is home. The learner puts the steps in order, opens the job aids, sorts parcels by their handling code, takes a phone call with the customer, writes a call to an AI customer, finds the safe place at the door and handles two stops that branch. The guide covers the tokens, the fonts, the components, motion, sound and the words on screen.

Every value comes from the module’s source: src/design/tokens.json, the single source of truth; base.css and contrast.css; the components in src/components/ui/, each with its own stylesheet; and the docs on the design system, the audio and the module. The component figures are screenshots of the module’s own design-document page.

Tokens first

Edit tokens.json, then run npm run tokens; it also runs before dev and build. scripts/build-tokens.js writes tokens.css, which is never edited by hand: a test fails if it drifts.

2Principles

PrincipleWhat it means on every screen
P1 · Readable on the streetDrivers read this outdoors, on a phone, between stops. Text is at least 16px (18px from 600px wide), body text reaches 7:1 contrast, and every control is at least 44 × 44px.
P2 · One thing per screenEach screen asks one question and has one primary action, pinned to the bottom. Anything else waits for the next screen or goes into the job aids.
P3 · Meaning never by color aloneEvery state carries an icon and a word as well as a color: “Correct” with a check, “Not quite” with a cross, “Selected” with a filled radio.

The source cites them by number: src/design/states.js, the list of every state and how it is shown besides color, points to P1 to P3, and two tests read that list (see Accessibility).

3Color

Light theme only. The palette has seventeen tokens, each with a role in tokens.json and a custom property --color-<name> in tokens.css. Contrast is WCAG 2 contrast, computed with src/design/contrast.js. Text needs 4.5:1 (AA) and body text aims for 7:1 (AAA); outlines and icons need 3:1.

  • Primary #1F4E8C carries the primary buttons, the selection and the focus ring, with white text at 8.31:1. Hover and pressed darken to primary-strong #173C6D (11.03:1). Primary-tint #E5EDF7 fills the selected choice and the driver’s bubble.
  • Accent #F2A900 is the single highlight per screen. It is a fill only, never text: ink reads on it at 7.77:1, but it reaches only 2.01:1 on surface. Accent-tint #FDF0CC is its soft fill.
  • Success and error mark correct or delivered and incorrect or violation, on icons and borders (5.35:1 and 6.57:1 on surface). Their tints fill the panels, with ink text at 13.40:1 and 13.23:1. Slate-tint #ECEEF1 does the same for info, returned and disabled.
  • Outline #7D828B draws the control outlines of choice cards and secondary buttons, at 3.86:1 on surface. Divider #E4E0D8 draws 1px dividers and level-1 borders.
  • Disabled text #646A73 sits on disabled controls. It carries an instruction (“Choose an option first”), so it reaches 4.69:1 on slate-tint.
Illustration

The doorstep picture on slide 6 uses an ink outline, flat fills from the palette and rounded joins, with no gradients. Outlines stay 2px at any size. Amber, green and red are not used in it: they are kept for the one highlight and for feedback.

4Type

Headings are Barlow Semi Condensed 600. Body text is Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 400 and 700. Sizes step by 1.25 from a root of 16px, which becomes 18px from 600px wide, and text blocks stop at 65ch. There are no italics: emphasis is bold, and base.css sets em, i, cite and the rest upright.

Codes and house numbers

Barlow’s O and 0, and its I and l, look alike. So codes and house numbers are always set in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, even inside a heading: wrap them in .ds-code, which switches the font and keeps the size and weight of the text around it. Atkinson has 400 and 700, so a 600 heading shows its code at 700.

Both fonts are self-hosted woff2 files, the latin and latin-ext subsets from Fontsource 5.3.0, with no CDN, so a SCORM package keeps working when an LMS blocks outside requests. Both are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and the license texts ship next to the fonts.

5Space, shape and layout

Space is on a 4px base, in eight steps from --space-1 to --space-8. Touch targets are at least 44 × 44px and at least 8px apart. Components size themselves from these steps: a button has 8px by 24px of padding, a flip card is at least 128px tall (two steps of 64), and the dialog is at most 768px wide (twelve).

Layout

Content is at most 960px wide, centered with token margins, on a grid of 4, 8 or 12 columns (.ds-container and .ds-grid in base.css). The root size changes with the first breakpoint, so all type grows by an eighth from 600px wide.

  • Under 600px4 columns16px side margin16px gutter16px root size
  • 600 to 839px8 columns24px side margin16px gutter18px root size
  • 840px and up12 columns32px side margin24px gutter18px root size

Screen anatomy

One slide on a 360px-wide phone, with an answer checked and the feedback panel open. The action bar holds exactly one primary button and, from slide 2, a text-style Back button. A slide the learner comes back to shows the checked answer again, locked.

6Icons

Lucide icons on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke, at 16, 20 and 24px only. An icon never stands alone: it always has a visible or an accessible text label. 35 of them are in use.

7Components

React components in src/components/ui/, styled only with the tokens. Each figure shows a component in its states, as the module draws it in a column about 360px wide, at the 18px root.

Button

Primary is filled; hover and pressed darken it to primary-strong. Secondary is outlined. Text has no fill or border and is kept for a secondary action, such as Back beside the one primary button. Every button is at least 44 × 44px with 8px corners, in bold body type. A disabled button still says what to do first, with an arrow and the words.

ChoiceCard

One option of a single-answer question (slides 4 and 7), as a radio: the arrow keys move between the options of a group. Each state shows an icon and a word as well as its color: a filled dot and “Selected”, a check and “Correct”, a cross and “Incorrect”.

FeedbackPanel and OutcomePanel

FeedbackPanel rises from the action bar after Check answer, with the level 2 shadow, a 2px colored top edge and a title with its icon: “Correct”, “Not quite” or “Good to know”. OutcomePanel shows how a stop ended, in the three endings of slide 7 and the results: Delivered, Policy violation and Returned to depot.

DialogueBubble, PlayLineButton and CaptionBar

The call on slide 4. Every bubble names its speaker; the customer speaks from the left beside an avatar, and the driver, “You”, from the right in primary-tint. PlayLineButton plays one spoken line. Nothing plays by itself (WCAG 1.4.2): the learner presses it. It sits in the bubble of the line it plays, and beside each line the learner can choose, so they can hear it first.

An <audio> element does not draw captions, so the app reads the WebVTT track and shows the active cue in the CaptionBar, with the speaker’s name. Captions are on by default; the toggle shows its state as an icon and a word.

ChatComposer and CheckCard

Slide 5, the AI chat. The composer is a labeled multi-line field with a character counter; Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line. Its send button is the screen’s primary button, in the ActionBar. When the AI is not available, the learner marks the call moves they made with CheckCards: a marked card shows a checked box and the word “Marked”.

TopBar and ActionBar

The TopBar shows progress as “3 of 7” over a bar, and the Job aids button, which waits until slide 2 and says so. The ActionBar holds the one primary button; until the learner has chosen, it says what to do first.

FlipCard, RevealCard and Dialog

A FlipCard turns over in place to show its back on primary-tint. A RevealCard holds a job aid (slide 2 and the Job aids dialog): its front is a disclosure button, and its back holds lists, so it turns in below the front instead of inside it. An opened card shows “Viewed” with an icon. The Dialog is a modal panel with the level 2 shadow, in the WAI-ARIA dialog pattern: focus moves to its title and stays inside; Esc, Close or a tap on the backdrop closes it, and focus returns to the button that opened it.

StepCard, ParcelCard, CodeBadge and Bin

A StepCard is one step in the sequencing list of slide 1. There are three ways to move it: drag the handle, use the up and down buttons, or tap it and then the step whose place it takes. With a keyboard, Space picks it up, the arrow keys move it and Space drops it. After checking, a step out of place says where it goes and what goes wrong in that order.

A ParcelCard on slide 3 shows its handling code as a CodeBadge, the stop note and the call. CodeBadge has one neutral style for every code: the icon and the word tell them apart, and the code is set in .ds-code. A Bin lists its parcels; while a parcel is selected, the bin’s heading becomes a button that says what it will do.

Doorstep scene

Slide 6. Flat shapes, 2px ink outlines and token colors only, with just the cues the safe-place rules need. Each numbered spot is a real 44px button placed in percent; the selected one wears the screen’s amber highlight, and a line under the picture names it.

8Motion

Short and plain: a state changes in 150ms, things enter in 250ms and cards turn in 300ms, all ease-out. With reduced motion every movement becomes a 150ms opacity fade.

Token and valueUse
--motion-state
150ms		State changes: the fill, border and text color of buttons and cards, and the width of the progress bar
--motion-enter
250ms		Entering: the feedback panel rises from behind the action bar, and the dialog rises 24px into place, both fading in
--motion-flip
300ms		Turning: the flip card turns over, and the back of a reveal card turns in below its front
--motion-ease
ease-out		Every movement
--motion-shift
1, or 0 with reduced motion		Multiplies every slide and turn, so at 0 they stay in place and only fade

Reduced motion comes from the system setting (prefers-reduced-motion), or is forced on a part of the page with data-motion="reduced". Then --motion-enter and --motion-flip become 150ms and --motion-shift 0. The flip card no longer rotates: its faces cross-fade. With full motion its faces swap at the half-turn, edge-on, where the swap is unseen.

9Sound and captions

Only slide 4 speaks. Each line of the call is a short MP3 with a WebVTT captions file next to it, made from the text in module.json by npm run tts. The speech is synthetic: no recording of a person is in it.

WhatDetail
VoicesPiper en_US-libritts_r-medium, a model with 904 speakers: speaker 1535 for the driver (“You”, a low voice) and 2254 for Ms. Rivera (a higher voice), both with lengthScale 1.15, a little slower
Files40 kbps mono MP3, one per line, named by line id: opening, <turn>-<choice> for the driver and <turn>-<choice>-reply for Ms. Rivera; all of slide 4 stays under a 2 MB limit
The ringThe phone ringing in the opening is a generated 440 + 480 Hz tone, so “[phone rings]” is what you hear
CaptionsThe speaker’s name at the start of a cue (“Ms. Rivera: Hello?”), at most 2 lines of about 42 characters per cue, and non-speech sounds in brackets
TimingEach sentence is spoken on its own, and its start and end come from the sample count plus the 50 ms an MP3 decoder adds, so a cue never drifts
  • No autoplay (WCAG 1.4.2). No <audio> exists until a Play button is pressed. One line plays at a time, and pressing it again stops it.
  • Captions on by default. Each playing clip carries a hidden <track kind="captions">; the app listens to its cue changes and shows the active cue in the caption bar. The toggle’s choice is kept for the session, so it holds on every slide and after a reload.
  • Your own voices. An audio file per line in scripts/tts/input/ always wins over Piper, and the captions are still generated from its length. With neither, the script stops and lists the lines to record: it never fakes audio.

10Voice and microcopy

The module talks to a driver between stops, one question per screen, in short, plain US English.

  • Buttons say the action: “Check answer”, “Show hint”, “Continue”, “Back”, “Close”, “Try again”, “Improve your score”, “Finish and submit”.
  • A disabled control says what to do first: “Choose an option first”, “Reach slide 2 first”.
  • States are words, each with its icon: “Selected”, “Correct”, “Incorrect”, “In place”, “Viewed”, “Marked”, “Done”, “Missed”, “Not reached”, “Opens again”, “Captions on”, “Captions off”, “Audio unavailable”.
  • Feedback names the result, then the rule: “Not quite” over “A SIG parcel needs a signature, even from a neighbor.” A misplaced item says where it goes: “Goes at step 3”, “Goes in: Pickup point”.
  • The endings are “Delivered”, “Policy violation” and “Returned to depot”.
  • Every bubble names its speaker (“You”, “Ms. Rivera”), and so does every caption cue.
  • A middle dot joins parts of a label, as in “SIG parcel · house 14”; progress reads “3 of 7”.
  • Moves are announced in a live region as each pick-up, move and drop happens: “Call the customer: step 3 of 6.”
  • Hints sit where they are needed: “Enter sends. Shift+Enter starts a new line.” and “Press Play on a line. Its captions appear here.”
  • Terms: handling code (STD, SIG, AGE), stop note, safe place, neighbor, pickup point, depot, call moves, job aids.

11Accessibility

Drivers use the module on a phone outdoors, so every task works with touch, a pointer and a keyboard, and moves are announced to screen readers. npm run test:a11y runs axe-core on every slide and state at 360, 600, 840 and 1280px.

Every way to move

Drag and drop on slides 1 and 3 runs on one machine, and every way of moving an item sends it the same events.

WayHow
Pointer or touchDrag the grip handle, which takes touch drags instead of scrolling. The page scrolls while you hold near its top or bottom edge, and Esc cancels.
Tap, no dragging (WCAG 2.5.7)Tap an item, which shows “Selected” with an icon, then tap the step whose place it takes, or a bin.
ButtonsThe up and down buttons on each step (slide 1).
Keyboard (WCAG 2.1.1)Tab to an item; Space picks it up, the arrow keys move it, Space drops it and Escape cancels.

On slide 6 a text list of the same five spots gives the same answer as the picture, and the figure carries a short description.

Contrast modes

The palette is designed for 7:1 on body text. Two settings of the device ask for more, and src/design/contrast.css honors both without a second palette: it only re-points tokens and uses system colors.

SettingWhat changes
prefers-contrast: moreOutlines, dividers and secondary text become ink (15.6:1 on white). Lines are 2px and control outlines 3px; the focus ring is 3px. Green and red text are darkened to 7:1 on every surface they sit on. The illustration keeps its palette.
forced-colors: activeThe browser replaces the colors with the user’s palette and removes shadows. A selected card gets a 3px border in Highlight; correct is solid and incorrect dashed; the picked marker on the picture has a double ring; disabled controls are GrayText with a dashed border; the focus ring is 3px in Highlight; the progress bar keeps its fill.
A state never relies on color alone

src/design/states.js lists every state and how it is shown besides color: an icon and a word, or a shape. states.test.js scans the component CSS and fails if a rule gives a state a color, border or shadow that is not in the list. A browser test walks slides 1 to 7 and fails if a state on screen has no icon or no visible word.
